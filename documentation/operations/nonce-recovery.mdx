---
title: "Ordered nonce recovery"
description: "Diagnose Endpoint Contract #11 and recover a packet blocked behind a missing predecessor."
---

On the deployed Stellar Endpoint, `Error(Contract, #11)` during `clear` means the destination tried to execute a packet with an invalid next nonce. The common case is that nonce `N + 1` reached execution while nonce `N` was still missing.

## Why verification is not enough

Packet verification and packet execution are separate states. A later packet can be verified and committed, but Endpoint ordering still prevents its `lz_receive` call until every earlier nonce on the same pathway has executed.

## Recovery procedure

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify the blocked pathway">
    Record the source EID, 32-byte sender, destination receiver, GUID, and nonce from the diagnostic event.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find the missing predecessor">
    Search source `PacketSent` events for the same sender/receiver pathway and nonce `N - 1`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify the predecessor">
    Submit its canonical packet header and payload hash through the configured DVN.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Commit the predecessor">
    After the ULN reports it verifiable, commit its verification on the destination.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Execute in order">
    Execute nonce `N - 1`, then retry nonce `N`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Relay behavior

The relay scans historical Sepolia `PacketSent` logs, serializes processing for the pathway, and automatically backfills missing predecessors. `SEPOLIA_SCAN_START_BLOCK=11488800` ensures the tested packet range remains discoverable after restart.

This behavior was validated with nonce 5: the relay found and executed missing nonce 4 first, then committed and executed nonce 5. The destination `received_message_count` reached `5`.

## Avoid unsafe shortcuts

Do not bypass peer checks or forge destination state to make counters match. Recovery should replay the authentic source packet through verification, commitment, and ordered Endpoint execution.
